Essential question: Why is less energy available to organisms at higher levels of a food pyramid?

Learning targets

  • Trace energy flow from the Sun to producers and consumers.
  • Use a food chain and food pyramid to explain energy transfer.
  • Interpret an illustrative 10% energy-transfer model.
  • Explain why energy is reduced at successive trophic levels.

I – INTENTIONS

Prior knowledge activation (5 minutes)

Ask: “What did you eat for breakfast? Where did the energy in that food originally come from?” Invite learners to trace one food item back to its source.

Objectives

  1. Identify the original energy source in most food chains.
  2. Describe how energy passes from producers to consumers.
  3. Compare the amount of energy available at lower and higher trophic levels.
  4. Explain the role of energy loss as heat and energy use in life processes.

Success criteria

Learners can correctly label a four-level energy pyramid, draw arrows in the correct direction, and explain the decrease in available energy in two or more sentences.

Key discussion

  • Producers capture light energy and store some of it as chemical energy in food.
  • Consumers obtain chemical energy by eating organisms.
  • Organisms use energy for movement, growth, repair and other life processes; some energy is transferred to the surroundings as heat.
  • Energy flows through an ecosystem; nutrients, unlike energy, can be recycled.

Part A – Complete the statements

  1. The original energy source for most ecosystems is the __________.
  2. Organisms that make their own food are called __________.
  3. Arrows in a food chain show the direction of __________ transfer.

Part B – Explain

  1. Why is the producer level usually the widest part of an energy pyramid?
  2. Why is less energy available at higher trophic levels?
  3. What may happen to frogs if grasshoppers become scarce? Explain.

Part C – Draw

Draw and label a four-level food chain from a familiar ecosystem. Include arrows showing energy transfer.

Answer key – teacher use

  1. Sun
  2. Producers
  3. Energy
  4. Producers capture energy and form the base supporting consumers.
  5. Organisms use energy for life processes and some is dissipated as heat; not all biomass/energy is passed on.
  6. Frog numbers may decrease because less food is available, although other factors also affect populations.

Teacher guide and assessment rubric

Materials

Computer/projector or printed infographic, board, paper, markers, organism cards, calculator (optional).

Misconceptions to address

  • Energy arrows point from the food source to the consumer, not toward the organism being eaten.
  • The 10% model is approximate; transfer efficiency varies.
  • Decomposers act on dead organisms and wastes from all trophic levels; they are not simply a single final step in every food chain.
  • Energy flows and is eventually dissipated; matter/nutrients are recycled.
